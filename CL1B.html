The History of the CL 1B

In 1987, Danish audio manufacturer TUBE-TECH introduced the CL 1A, an all-tube optical compressor designed by company founder John G. Petersen. The unit was developed during a period when interest in vintage tube equipment was growing, inspiring Petersen to create high-quality recording equipment of his own.

Petersen's approach focused on simple circuit designs, carefully selected components, and the distinctive sound of tube-based amplification. Drawing inspiration from classic studio equipment, he developed a compressor that combined smooth optical gain reduction with the warmth and musicality of an all-tube signal path.

In 1991, TUBE-TECH introduced the CL 1B, a successor to the original CL 1A that retained its fundamental design while incorporating circuit boards in place of the original hardwired construction.

The CL 1B quickly established itself as one of TUBE-TECH's signature products, becoming particularly well known for its smooth, transparent compression and ability to control dynamics without sacrificing the character of the original performance.

Explore the CL 1B Legacy

Over the years, the TUBE-TECH CL 1B has become a studio standard, especially for vocal recording. Its optical gain reduction system and all-tube amplification deliver a smooth, controlled sound while preserving the clarity and natural character of the source.

Unlike many compressors, the CL 1B offers continuously adjustable attack and release times, alongside variable ratio and threshold controls. This combination gives engineers the flexibility to shape compression precisely, from subtle dynamic control to more pronounced compression and saturation.

Although the CL 1B is particularly popular for vocals, it is also widely used on bass, guitars, keyboards, and other instruments. Its distinctive character has made it a valued tool across a wide range of musical genres.

Today, the CL 1B remains one of TUBE-TECH's most recognizable designs. Its legacy has also extended into the digital world, with software versions bringing the characteristic sound and functionality of the original hardware to modern recording studios.
